CBRE / GWS

CBRE is the world's largest commercial real estate services firm. The Global Workplace Solutions team had a high-stakes demo coming up — and the standard demo environment didn't tell the story they needed to tell.

Clickable Demo Prototype Deck
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
orientation → deep research → data extraction → visual hunt → claude handoff
"The CBRE TransAct demo storyboarding feels like a mess — there were starts and stops and then more information from the customer. Review the channel and files diligently… come back with an end-to-end storyboard."
Started by mapping every user role across all CBRE channels (brokers, TMs, analysts). Then pushed Slackbot to reconcile fractured notes into a clean storyboard — that storyboard was the brief I handed to Claude.
What I Fed Claude
📸Screenshots of their existing FM platform
📝Discovery call notes and stakeholder themes
🎯The story I needed to tell in the demo
🏢CBRE GWS portfolio context and scale
The Journey
1
Screenshots instead of a spec

Dropped in screenshots of their current FM platform and asked Claude to understand what the user was actually trying to do inside it.

the prompt that mattered"Here's what their current system looks like. What story is this person trying to tell?" — no brief, no wireframe. Just context and an outcome question.
2
Outcomes, not interfaces

Redirected Claude away from building screens and toward the experience — what a great day looks like for a GWS facility manager.

the prompt that mattered"Don't build me a UI. Tell me what a great day looks like for a GWS facility manager — the outcomes, not the screens." That reframe is what made the demo feel like theirs.
3
One redirect rebuilt the entire UX hierarchy

When the first version buried the most important signal, I pushed back — and Claude reorganized everything around it.

the prompt that mattered"Lead with the maintenance alert. The FM manager shouldn't have to dig for it." One sentence. Claude rebuilt the information hierarchy from scratch.
4
Made it feel unmistakably CBRE

Asked Claude to tune every data point — terminology, portfolio scale, operational language — until it stopped feeling like a demo and started feeling like their system.

Equinox

Equinox is one of the most recognized premium fitness and lifestyle brands in the world. The FY27 SIC — Strategic Investment Council — was approaching. This is the highest-stakes internal meeting of the year: you're pitching the investment case for your account to Salesforce leadership.

Deck Executive POV
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
strategic framing → persona-led build → constraint-first → SIC validation
"Act as a distinguished SE and wear your best hat. Review all Equinox intel in channels, DMs, Google Drive. Build me an agenda, deck outline, and supporting talk tracks. Be specific because I am going to give this to Claude."
The constraint that shaped the entire SIC came from this session: not a new vision — unlock the HOW. Slackbot established the strategic angle before Claude saw a word.
What I Fed Claude
🏋️Equinox brand and business context
💼Current deal status and stakeholder map
📋SIC format and what leadership expects
🤖Agentforce capabilities relevant to fitness/luxury
The Journey
1
Asked Claude to challenge my assumptions first

Before building a single slide, I asked Claude to push back on my narrative — not confirm it. That changed everything that came after.

the prompt that mattered"Before you build anything — challenge my assumptions about what Equinox actually needs from this investment." Claude came back with questions I hadn't thought to ask.
2
Built the arc: why now, why this, why inevitable

The transformation story had to feel like something Equinox was already moving toward — not a pitch we were making. Claude helped make the case feel like a conclusion, not an ask.

3
Wrote for the room, not the slide deck

Not talking points to read — actual language for a Salesforce leadership audience mid-interruption. The recovery lines. The pauses. The proof points that land at this level.

the prompt that mattered"Write this for someone who will be interrupted mid-slide by a CFO. Give me the recovery line for each transition." That level of specificity completely changed the output.
4
"Can you make this interactive?" → shareable demo, same session

One question. Claude turned the key concepts into a live, clickable experience without needing a new brief or starting over.

Service Logic

Service Logic is a Bain Capital portfolio company — 60 operating companies, $1B+ in HVAC and mechanical services revenue, and heading toward an IPO. They issued a formal FSM RFP. They had already rejected ServiceMax. The bar was high and the stakes were real.

Complex RFP SE Playbook Solution Architecture
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
cold read → strategic advisor → structured deliverable → polish
"Read the RFP in its entirety as if you are a distinguished SE with an eye for strategic questions, 'gotchas,' and overall guidance for how we can ensure we win — act as a consultative advisor."
Dropped a 90-page RFP, response template, Google folder, and Slack channel. Slackbot read it all before Claude did — the strategic framing, what to win on, and what to watch for came from this pass.
What I Fed Claude
📄The full 100+ page RFP document
⚠️Why ServiceMax was previously rejected
🏗️Context on their 60-OpCo multi-entity structure
📊ASC 606 / IPO readiness as a known Must-have
The Journey
1
Mapped every requirement as a deal risk, not a feature

Instead of asking "do we cover this," I asked Claude to flag each gap in terms of whether it was the kind of thing that loses deals. Different framing, completely different output.

the prompt that mattered"Don't just tell me what we cover. For every gap, tell me how it reads as deal risk — and whether it's the kind of thing that loses RFPs." That instruction changed how Claude organized its entire analysis.
2
Pushed Claude past the gap — and into the proof point

When Claude flagged ASC 606, I didn't stop there. I pushed: is this a product gap or a positioning gap? That follow-up is what surfaced RightRev and CBRE as the answer.

the prompt that mattered"You flagged ASC 606. But tell me — is this a product gap or a positioning gap? And if I lead with RightRev, what's my proof point?" Claude connected it to the CBRE deal.
3
Built the SE Playbook: what to say AND what to avoid

Not just the solution — the positioning. Asked Claude for the language that lands with a PE-backed leadership team heading toward an IPO. Different audience, completely different register.

4
Solution Architecture mapped to their actual org model

60 OpCos, multi-entity structure, IPO readiness. Asked Claude to map the solution to HOW Service Logic actually operates — not a generic architecture slide.

GSA PBS

GSA Public Buildings Service is the federal government's real estate arm — managing billions in property across the country. Federal accounts have specific procurement rules, formal stakeholder hierarchies, and very little tolerance for a pitch that doesn't feel tailored to government reality.

Deck Talk Track Points
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
targeted research → asset build → talk track
"Find the best relevant use case success stories for GSA — CBRE, Lennar, RBC — with the heaviest hitting stats or quotes featured for each."
Named the accounts, asked for the proof points that would land hardest in a federal room. The stats and quotes Slackbot surfaced went directly into the deck and talk track points.
What I Fed Claude
🏛️GSA PBS account context and mission
👥Attendee list with roles and seniority
📐Meeting agenda structure and time constraints
💡Notes on what had resonated in prior meetings
The Journey
1
Asked what should HAPPEN — not what to present

That framing shift caused Claude to push back before building anything. It came back with questions about who was in the room and what each person needed to walk out believing.

the prompt that mattered"What should actually happen in the first 20 minutes?" — not "what should I say." The outcome framing changed the entire response. Claude interrogated the room before it touched the slides.
2
Federal framing without having to explain it

Mission impact first. Not product features. I told Claude this was a federal room — and it adjusted the language, proof points, and ROI framing without needing a primer on government selling.

3
Precision prompting produced precision output

The more specific I got about the format, the more useful the output became. Asking for exact transitions and recovery lines — not just bullets — is what turned this from a prep doc into a real guide.

the prompt that mattered"Give me the exact words for the transition between slides 4 and 5. And tell me where to pause for the procurement person's question." That precision completely changed what came back.
4
A printed guide that went into the room

Not to read verbatim. To walk in knowing exactly what I was going to say — and how to handle the moment it went off script.

Clickable Demo Prototype
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
story dreaming → time-boxing → editorial → promise check → rescue
"Act as a distinguished SE who might need to be a hero and save a demo tomorrow — give me an output I can share with Claude."
Six Slackbot sessions on this one — from story dreaming to time-boxing to a night-before rescue pass. The handoff was explicit: "give me something I can hand to Claude."
What I Fed Claude
🏘️Related Rentals company profile and portfolio
😤Core pain points: tenant comms, vendor mgmt, leases
👤Day-in-the-life of a property manager persona

The Journey
1
The anchor sentence came before anything else

Before asking for a demo, I asked Claude for the one sentence a property manager would say on a Monday morning that describes exactly why their job is hard. That sentence became the entire narrative hook.

the prompt that mattered"Give me one sentence that a property manager would say to their manager on a Monday morning about why their job is hard." That sentence built both demos.
2
Built the baseline first — the world as it is today

Showed the fragmented workflow before any AI. A realistic baseline they'd recognize. The contrast only works if you earn it.

3
One sentence. Second demo. No new brief.

The context was already established. The second demo didn't need a new conversation — just one follow-up that redirected Claude into building the AI-powered version of everything it had just built.

the prompt that mattered"Now show what happens when AI touches this." That was the entire prompt for the second demo. Claude built it from one sentence because the foundation was already there.
4
Team reviewed both before the meeting — same session

Shared the live demos with the AE for feedback. Made tweaks in real time. No handoffs, no 2-week dev request, no waiting.

Hertz

Hertz is one of the world's most iconic rental car brands — 100+ years old, operating at massive scale, and in the middle of a significant transformation. They needed to see Agentforce not as a new AI tool sitting next to their existing systems, but as something living inside the screens their service agents already use every day.

Clickable Demo Prototype
dab ⚡
First stop, slackbot
discovery intel → demo story → use case mapping → claude handoff
"Be my storyteller and demo dreamer. Review the following notes and this demo video — outline a clear, compelling story for how Agentforce can help that we can demo."
Pulled Lauryl's account context, prior touchpoints, and positioning threads. The "AI in the flow of work, not a separate tool" framing came directly from this research pass.
What I Fed Claude
🟡Hertz brand colors (iconic yellow #FFD100, black)
🎫Service agent workflow context and case types
🚗Real use case: damage dispute, rental return scenario
🎯The story — AI in the flow of work, not a separate tool
👤Specific stakeholder context (Lauryl)
The Journey
1
Told Claude to start in the problem, not the solution

Instead of jumping straight to AI, I asked Claude to design a reveal that started where Hertz already was. The contrast only lands if you earn the before.

the prompt that mattered"Don't start with AI. Start where Hertz is today — let the room feel the problem before they see the answer." That instruction built the entire three-act structure.
2
Named the three acts before building any of them

"Governed Foundation → AI in the Flow → One Platform." I gave Claude the arc first. Once the narrative had names, every UI decision had a reason.

3
Specificity made it real: a Tesla dispute at LAX

Not a generic service case — a real scenario. Real case type, real priority queue, real data fields. The more specific the scenario I gave, the more recognizable the output became.

the prompt that mattered"Use a Tesla damage dispute at LAX return. Real fields. Real priority." Swapping generic for specific completely changed how the demo felt in the room.
4
"Revise based on the story — and make it unmistakably Hertz"

That was the entire final direction. Claude dialed the Hertz yellow into every detail — the nav, the glow, the accents — until it felt like their product, not a mockup of it.
